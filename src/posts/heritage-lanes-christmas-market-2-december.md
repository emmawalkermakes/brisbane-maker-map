Most Christmas markets ask for your weekend or your Friday night. This one asks for your lunch break. The Market Folk's **Heritage Lanes Christmas Market** is on a Wednesday in the middle of the CBD, and it runs for just three hours. That makes it handy for city workers, and slightly tricky: you need a plan to get much done in a 30- or 60-minute break.

Details below are from The Market Folk's [Heritage Lanes Christmas Market page](https://themarketfolk.com.au/pages/heritage-lanes-christmas-market-2026), checked on 19 November 2026.

> **Check the organiser's page before you go.** Times and details can change, and the stallholder line-up hasn't been published yet.

## The essentials

- **When:** Wednesday 2 December 2026, 11.30am to 2.30pm
- **Where:** Heritage Lanes, 80 Ann Street, Brisbane City QLD 4000
- **Entry:** Free, no ticket or booking needed
- **What's on:** a curated line-up of local makers with Christmas gifting in mind. The organiser lists jewellery, ceramics, candles, art, homewares, fashion and gourmet treats, and says every stallholder is handpicked.
- **Line-up:** to be shared on The Market Folk's blog and Instagram (@themarketfolk) closer to the day.

One difference from their other Christmas markets: The Market Folk's list of dog-friendly Christmas markets names King Street, Boggo Road Village, Gasworks Plaza and Portside Wharf, but not Heritage Lanes. If you were planning to bring a dog, one of those is the better fit.

## Making a lunch break count

Three hours of trading doesn't mean three hours for you. If you've got a standard lunch break, here's how to make it work.

### Before the day

- **Write a short list.** Two or three people you'd like to find something for, and a rough budget for each. Without a list, a half-hour lap can disappear in browsing.
- **Watch for the line-up.** When The Market Folk post the stallholders, look up the ones that catch your eye. Knowing you want "the ceramics stall" or "the candle maker" saves a lot of wandering.
- **Check how far 80 Ann Street is from your office.** Use a map app to time the walk both ways and subtract it from your break.
- **Bring a bag.** A foldable tote in your work bag means you're not juggling parcels back to your desk.

### On the day

- **Go early if you can.** The organiser's general advice for their Christmas markets is to arrive early for the best pick of one-off and limited pieces. An 11.30am start, if your break allows it, gives you the fullest tables.
- **Do one lap before you buy.** On a short break it's tempting to buy at the first stall. A quick walk around first, then a return to the two or three that stood out, usually ends in better choices.
- **Ask about custom or later orders.** If a maker has what you want but not in the right colour or size, ask whether they take orders or have an online shop. You don't need to fit every decision into one lunch hour.
- **Eat while you walk, or after.** If your break is tight, grab lunch on the way back rather than queueing first.

### If you can only spare 20 minutes

Pick one person on your list, go straight for the type of stall that suits them, and buy the best thing you find. One great gift ticked off is still a win.

## Buying gifts to carry back to the office

A few practical things about shopping in work clothes, mid-week:

- **Fragile things:** ask the maker to wrap ceramics or glass well, and carry them upright. Many makers have tissue or bubble wrap on hand.
- **Candles and chocolate in December heat:** don't leave them on a sunny windowsill at your desk or in a hot car on the drive home.
- **Gifts for colleagues:** if you're buying for a work Kris Kringle, it's a good chance to pick up something handmade and local without a separate weekend trip.
- **Payment:** the organiser's event page doesn't cover payment methods. Many market stalls take card, but carrying a little cash is a sensible backup at any market.

## Getting there

The organiser lists the address as 80 Ann Street, Brisbane City. If you're coming from further away, plan your trip with the [Translink journey planner](https://jp.translink.com.au/). For anyone working in the CBD, walking is likely to be the simplest option.

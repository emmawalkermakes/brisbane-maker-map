A handmade gift often needs a bit more care than its mass-produced cousin. Handmade isn't fragile — just different. A hand-thrown mug may or may not be dishwasher safe. A hand-poured candle burns best when it's lit the right way the first time. A timber board needs oil now and then.

Giving a little care note with the gift makes it last longer and stops the recipient from finding out the hard way. Below is a practical guide by material, plus the questions worth asking a maker at the stall before you buy.

## The golden rule: ask the maker

The person who made the piece knows it best. Before you pay, ask:

- "How should this be cleaned?"
- "Is it safe for the dishwasher / microwave / oven / outdoors?" (whichever applies)
- "Is there anything that will damage it?"
- "Do you have a care card I could include with the gift?"

Many makers include care cards already, or have care information on their website. If they do, keep it with the gift. The general tips below are a backup, not a replacement for the maker's advice.

## Ceramics

Handmade mugs, bowls, plates and vases are among the most popular market gifts, and also the most likely to come with specific instructions.

- **Dishwasher and microwave use varies.** Some handmade ceramics are fine in both; others aren't. Ask the maker, and pass the answer on.
- **Metallic glazes and lustres don't go in the microwave.** Gold, silver or other metallic details can spark. Most makers will tell you this, but it's worth repeating in a care note.
- **Avoid sudden temperature changes.** Pouring boiling water into a cold mug straight from the fridge, or putting a hot dish on a cold, wet bench, can crack ceramics. Let pieces adjust gradually.
- **Check what it's for.** Some decorative pieces aren't meant to hold food or water. A vase that isn't glazed inside may let water seep through. If you're unsure, ask whether it's "food safe" and "watertight".

## Candles

A candle seems like the simplest gift, but a few habits make a big difference to how well it burns.

- **The first burn matters.** Most candle makers recommend letting a new candle burn until the melted wax reaches the edges of the container. If it's blown out too early, it can "tunnel" down the middle and waste wax for the rest of its life. That can take a while, so it's best lit when you'll be home for a few hours.
- **Trim the wick.** A short wick burns cleaner and calmer. Many makers suggest trimming it to around 5 mm before each lighting, but follow the maker's instructions.
- **Don't burn it for too long in one go.** Many makers suggest no more than a few hours at a time.
- **Never leave a burning candle unattended,** and keep it away from curtains, kids, pets and drafts.
- **Keep it out of the heat.** In a Brisbane summer, a candle left on a sunny windowsill or in a hot car can soften, sweat or warp. Store it somewhere cool.

## Jewellery

- **Sterling silver tarnishes** with exposure to air and moisture. Storing it in a small zip-lock bag or an airtight box slows tarnishing down, and a soft polishing cloth brings back the shine.
- **Take it off for swimming,** whether pool or ocean. Chlorine and salt water can be hard on metals and some stones.
- **Put jewellery on last.** Sunscreen, perfume, hairspray and moisturiser can dull metals and some stones over time. Apply them first, let them dry, then put jewellery on.
- **Ask about materials.** Plated pieces, natural stones, resin and polymer clay all need slightly different care. The maker can tell you what the piece is made of and how to clean it.

## Timber

Wooden chopping boards, serving boards, spoons and bowls are classic market finds.

- **Hand wash only,** with warm soapy water, and dry straight away. Don't soak timber or put it in the dishwasher, as it can crack or warp.
- **Oil it now and then.** When a board starts to look dry or pale, a food-safe oil helps protect it. Many makers sell their own board oil or wax, or can recommend one.
- **Let it dry standing up** or on its side, so air reaches both faces.
- **Watch for mould in humid weather.** In a Brisbane summer, store timber somewhere with airflow, not in a closed, damp cupboard straight after washing.

## Textiles

Hand-dyed tea towels, scarves, quilts, knitted pieces and clothing often need gentler washing than shop-bought equivalents.

- **Follow the label or care card.** If there isn't one, ask the maker.
- **Wash hand-dyed items separately at first.** Some dyes can bleed in the first few washes, so cold water and a separate load are a safe start.
- **Hand-knits** usually prefer a gentle hand wash and drying flat, so they don't stretch out of shape.
- **Dry out of harsh sun** if the colours matter. Strong sunlight can fade natural and hand-applied dyes over time.

## Art and prints

- **Hang away from direct sunlight.** UV light fades many inks, paints and papers.
- **Avoid damp walls and bathrooms** for works on paper; humidity can cause paper to ripple or develop spots.
- **Ask whether the print is archival,** and whether the maker recommends a particular framing approach.

## Handmade soap and skincare

- **Keep soap dry between uses.** A draining soap dish helps a handmade bar last much longer than one left sitting in water.
- **Check the use-by or best-before guidance.** Natural products without strong preservatives may have a shorter shelf life.
- **Store skincare out of the heat** and away from bathroom steam where possible.

## How to give a care note

A care note doesn't need to be fancy. A few lines on a gift tag or the back of a card is plenty:

> *Handmade in Brisbane by [maker's name]. Hand wash only — not for the microwave. Enjoy your morning coffee!*

Including the maker's name has a bonus: if the recipient loves it, they know who to go back to. That's one of the nicest things a handmade gift can do.

From now until Christmas, Brisbane's market calendar is the busiest it gets all year. It's also the time of year when the weather is working hardest against you: high UV, heavy humidity, and afternoon storms that can roll in from a clear sky in under an hour.

I love a handmade market, but it's very easy to end up leaving one at 11am, sunburnt, with a beeswax candle softening in a paper bag. So this isn't a list of markets (the [market calendar](/markets/) has those, with links to each organiser). It's how to actually enjoy them between now and February.

## Pick your time slot first

The single biggest decision is *when* you go, and it matters more in Brisbane summer than which market you choose.

**Early morning (opening to about 9.30am).** The coolest and calmest window. Stallholders are fresh and have time to chat, and the best one-off pieces haven't sold yet. The trade-off: some makers are still setting up in the first half-hour, so don't judge a market by its first ten minutes.

**Late morning to mid-afternoon.** The hardest slot. Peak UV, peak crowds, and in storm season, the part of the day when you start eyeing the clouds. If this is the only time you can go, pick a market with shade or cover, and keep the visit short.

**Twilight (late afternoon into evening).** My favourite for the summer months. It's cooler once the sun drops, there's usually food and music, and the lights make handmade stalls look their best. Afternoon storms are the main risk, which brings me to the next point.

## Watch the sky (and the radar)

South-east Queensland's storm season brings fast-developing afternoon and evening storms through the warmer months, and some are severe, with hail and damaging winds. Outdoor markets can close early or be cancelled on the day.

Before you head out:

- **Check the [Bureau of Meteorology](https://www.bom.gov.au/qld/) forecast and warnings**, and have a look at the radar if storms are forecast.
- **Check the organiser's page or social media on the day.** That's where cancellations and early closes are announced, not on third-party event listings.
- **Know where your shelter is.** A tent stall is not shelter in a severe storm. Know where the nearest building, car park or covered walkway is.

If a storm warning comes through while you're there, leave early. The market will be back next month; your phone and that ceramic mug might not survive a hailstorm.

> **Check the organiser's page before you go.** Times, locations and weather calls change, and the organiser's own page is the only reliable source.

## What I pack for a summer market

This is the list that lives in the tote bag by the door from November to March:

- **A hat with a real brim.** Caps leave your ears and neck exposed.
- **Sunscreen**, applied before you leave and topped up if you're out for more than a couple of hours.
- **A refillable water bottle**, frozen half-full the night before and topped up with water in the morning. Many markets have somewhere to refill, but not all.
- **A small towel or bandana.** For your neck, a damp seat, or wrapping a fragile buy.
- **A compact umbrella.** Doubles as sun shade.
- **A sturdy tote and a few cloth bags** (more on carrying things below).
- **A cooler bag with an ice brick**, if you're planning to buy anything heat-sensitive.
- **A little cash.** Most stallholders take cards now, but a few don't, and market wifi and mobile reception can be patchy in a crowd.

The [Cancer Council's SunSmart app](https://www.sunsmart.com.au/resources/sunsmart-app) shows the day's UV forecast and the times sun protection is recommended. In Brisbane in summer, that recommended window covers most of the day, which is a good reminder of why the early and twilight slots are worth it.

## Buying handmade in the heat

Handmade goods and Brisbane heat don't always get along. A few lessons:

**Candles, wax melts and beeswax wraps.** Soy and beeswax soften in a hot car very quickly. Buy these at the end of your visit, keep them out of direct sun, and never leave them in the boot while you go for lunch. A cooler bag works.

**Chocolate, fudge and baked goods.** Same rule. Last stop, cooler bag, straight home.

**Natural skincare and soaps.** Balms and butters can melt; bar soaps are usually fine. Ask the maker how they recommend storing it in summer. Good makers will tell you honestly.

**Ceramics and glass.** The heat isn't the problem; the crowds are. Ask the stallholder to wrap it well, and carry it in your hand rather than at the bottom of a tote under a pineapple.

**Prints and paper goods.** Humidity and a sweaty arm will warp a print fast. Ask for a backing board or a cardboard sleeve, and don't roll a print tightly.

**Textiles.** Generally heat-proof. But in humid weather, if you buy natural-fibre clothes or linen, give them a wash and dry before wearing, and check the care label.

### Ask before you pay

Summer is gift season, so it's worth asking a couple of questions at the stall:

- **Can it be personalised or made to order, and how long does that take?** Many makers take commissions at markets in November, but their Christmas cut-off may be earlier than you'd think.
- **Do they post?** Handy if you're buying a gift for someone interstate. (If you are, check Australia Post's Christmas cut-off dates too.)
- **Do they have a website or a regular stall** where you can find them again? Most makers have a card or a sticker on the back of the price tag. Take one.

## Getting there and home

Parking near inner-city markets on a summer Saturday can be its own small ordeal, and nobody wants to carry three bags and a pot plant ten blocks in 32 degrees.

- **Public transport or a lift** takes the parking stress out, especially for twilight markets in the city or South Bank. Translink's journey planner is the easiest way to check routes and times.
- **If you drive, park in shade or under cover** if you can, and pop sunshades up. It helps your purchases as well as the steering wheel.
- **Carry a fold-up trolley or a backpack** if you know you'll buy a lot. Your shoulders will thank you.

## Look after yourself, too

It sounds obvious, but in humid heat it's easy to underestimate how much you're sweating:

- **Drink before you're thirsty.** By the time you notice, you're behind.
- **Take a shade break** every half-hour or so. Most markets have a tree, a food truck area or an awning.
- **Know when to call it.** Dizziness, a headache or nausea in the heat are signals to stop, get into the shade or air-conditioning, and drink water. Severe symptoms need medical help.
- **Bring kids and dogs only if the market suits them.** Not every market allows dogs (the [market calendar](/markets/) notes where the organiser says so), and hot pavement is hard on paws.

## A simple summer market routine

If you want the short version, this is what I do:

1. Check the forecast and the organiser's page the night before and the morning of.
2. Freeze the water bottle, pack the hat, sunscreen and cooler bag.
3. Arrive close to opening or go to a twilight session.
4. Do one quick lap first to see everything, then a second lap to buy.
5. Heat-sensitive things last, straight into the cooler bag.
6. Leave before the clouds turn purple.

Summer is when Brisbane's makers bring their best stock out for Christmas, so it really is worth going. Just go early, go late, and bring a hat. For more ideas on filling a weekend, see my [Brisbane weekend for makers](/posts/brisbane-weekend-for-makers/) guide.

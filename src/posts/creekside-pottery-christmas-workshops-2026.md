There's a catch with handmade ceramic gifts that nobody mentions until it's too late: the kiln. Clay has to dry, be fired, be glazed and be fired again, and that takes weeks, not days. If you'd like to make a clay Christmas gift yourself, the only sensible way to plan is backwards from the day you want to hand it over.

Creekside Pottery Studio in Samford Valley, on Brisbane's north-western edge, is running a run of Christmas-themed hand-building workshops through November and December. I've read through the studio's workshop page as it stood on 5 November 2026, and this post lays the dates out with the firing wait worked into them.

> **Check the organiser's page before you go.** Places are limited, dates and availability come straight from the studio's booking system and can change. Everything below is from the [Creekside Pottery workshops page](https://creeksidepottery.com.au/workshops/), read on 5 November 2026.

## How the studio's process works

The page describes these as themed hand-building workshops, with every step guided, so you don't need experience. A few details matter for planning:

- **Clay, tools, tuition, glazes and kiln firings are included** in the price.
- **Your piece isn't finished on the night.** After it dries, it's bisque fired, glazed and fired again. The studio says finished pieces are *usually ready to collect within 2 to 3 weeks*, and you get a message when they're ready.
- **Suitable for adults and children over ten**, and each child needs their own booking.
- **Wine, soft drinks and snacks are provided** at the adult workshops. The studio is described as gluten and nut free on the gnome workshop listings.
- Most of the evening and afternoon workshops are listed at $99 per person on the day I checked.

The most useful line is the collection time. Count forward two to three weeks from the workshop date, and you've got your real "finished by" window.

A couple of things stand out when you set it out like this.

**The 4 December gingerbread house is cutting it fine.** Two to three weeks from 4 December lands you between 18 and 25 December. That's fine if you're making it for your own table, but it's risky as a gift for someone you're seeing on the 24th or 25th. If a gift is the goal, book earlier.

**The last workshop, Christmas Plates on 12 December, is a special case.** By the studio's usual two to three weeks, that would run past Christmas, but the listing itself says pieces will be ready to take home in time for Christmas. If you're booking that one for a gift, it's worth messaging the studio to confirm the exact collection date before you pay.

**The gnomes take longer.** The gnome listings mention a longer collection time than the usual two to three weeks, and the kids' gnome workshop says three to four weeks. That points to mid-to-late December for the 27 November session, which is workable but not relaxed. Ask the studio for a firm date.

## Which workshop suits what you're making

Because the studio lists them with descriptions, you can match the workshop to the occasion instead of just picking a date.

**For a table centrepiece:** the Christmas Candle workshop (20 November) has you hand-build two clay Christmas candles and decorate them with patterns, textures and colours. The Christmas Tree workshop (21 November) lets you design a tree of any style, tall and slim or short and plump, with baubles, garlands or presents around the base.

**For a gift to someone who loves a quirky decoration:** the gnome trio is three hand-built gnomes in three sizes, painted with underglaze colours and finished with a clear gloss glaze. The reindeer is a round, goofy figure with googly eyes and a red nose. Both are, by the studio's own description, built for personality over polish.

**For something to keep long-term:** the Christmas Plates workshop has you shape a plate over a press former or cut one from a rolled slab, add texture with rollers and stamps, then build festive details on top. The studio notes it's the last workshop of the year.

**For something a bit different:** the Kokedama Vessel workshop is the odd one out. It's Part 1 of a two-part set: you make a plaited or woven clay vessel at Creekside, and in Part 2 at a separate venue, Foliage and Moss, you make the kokedama (a ball of moss and greenery) that goes in it. Part 2 happens after your vessel has been fired, so you'll need to wait for that before the second session.

**For kids:** the Kids Christmas Gnome workshop on Saturday 28 November (9am to 10.30am) is for ages 6 to 14, and children under 8 need a parent or carer to stay. The studio asks that no food is brought to the session.

## A few practical tips

- **Book as soon as you've decided.** The page says places are kept small so everyone gets plenty of guidance, and the 7 November session already shows as sold out. Don't leave it until the week before.
- **Think about how you'll collect.** The studio is in Samford Valley, so a dedicated trip to pick up your piece is part of the plan. Ask whether someone else can collect it for you if you're going to be busy.
- **Check the postage options if the gift is going interstate.** I couldn't find any postage information on the page, so ask. Fired and glazed ceramics are breakable, and you'll want to package them properly.
- **Wear clothes that can handle clay.** The studio specifically asks for this, plus Christmas jumpers and tinsel are welcome at the festive sessions.
- **Don't rely on a perfect result.** The studio itself describes the reindeer and gingerbread house as wonky, goofy or whimsical. That's part of the charm, and probably why people keep them for years.

## The bottom line

If you want a kiln-fired Christmas ceramic as a gift, the real deadline isn't Christmas, it's about the first week of December. Book the 13, 20 or 21 November workshops and you should be collecting before the 12th. Later sessions are lovely for pieces you plan to keep yourself, as long as you don't mind the collection falling close to the day.

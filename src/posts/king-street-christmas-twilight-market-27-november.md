## What is locked in

A few details are clear enough to plan around now.

- **When:** Friday 27 November 2026, **4pm to 9pm**. That five-hour window matters. The Market Folk's other twilight Christmas markets — later dates at Boggo Road Village, Gasworks Plaza and Portside Wharf — are listed as 4pm to 8pm. King Street runs until nine, which helps if you finish work after five or want dinner first.
- **Where:** King Street, Bowen Hills QLD 4006. The organiser does not give a street number or meeting point, so use the address they published and check again if a more precise map appears.
- **Entry:** Free. No ticket and no booking.
- **Dogs:** Welcome on a lead.
- **The idea of the night:** The Market Folk describe this as the opening market of their Christmas season: finish work, grab dinner in the precinct, and get a head start on gift shopping under the evening lights.

The night is meant to sit alongside an ordinary Friday, not replace your whole weekend.

## How to spend the Friday evening

The 4pm-to-9pm span lets you shape the night around a normal finish time.

**Leave work close to four** if you want the "best pick" window the organiser points to. One-off jewellery, a favourite glaze or a limited print tend to go first. Do a slow lap, then decide; dinner can come afterwards.

**Finish closer to five or six** and you still have three to four hours. The Market Folk mention grabbing dinner in the precinct but do not name venues, so see what is open on King Street that evening rather than relying on an old list.

**Eat first, then shop** if you prefer the cooler, later part of the evening. A 9pm close makes that realistic, with the usual trade-off that a favourite piece may already have sold.

**If you only have an hour**, choose two or three people you still need gifts for, walk the stalls once, and leave. A short visit is kinder than a rushed lap at 8.50pm with a softening candle in a thin bag.

They have not listed entertainers, DJs or giveaways on the King Street page, so I have not invented any. If something extra is added later, it will show up on their channels.

## Shopping without turning it into a treasure hunt

A curated makers' market is different from a shopping-centre Christmas hall. You are usually talking to the person who made the work, which is the point — and also why a little preparation helps.

Write a short list rather than a spreadsheet: names, a rough budget, and one or two clues ("something for a new kitchen", "a small gift that can go in the post"). Leave room for the piece you did not plan to find.

Ask the questions that save January disappointment. Will this mug go in the dishwasher? Is this candle sensible around pets or strong summer heat? Can a piece of jewellery be resized? If you need something personalised, ask about December lead times before you fall for the sample on the table.

Think about the trip home before you pay. Ceramics, framed art and candles travel badly in a floppy bag. The organiser suggests bringing a reusable bag; I would take one with a flat base, plus a soft cloth for anything fragile. Keep gourmet treats and candles out of a hot car.

Most makers take cards, but outdoor precincts can have patchy reception when a market is busy. A little cash as backup helps. If a stall is three-deep with custom-order questions, take a card or note the maker's name and follow up later. Once the line-up is published, screenshot names before you go.

## Bringing a dog

Dogs on a lead are welcome at King Street, as they are at all seven of The Market Folk's branded Christmas markets.

A brief contrast, because it catches people out: The Market Folk also run markets at Riverlink in Ipswich, listed separately from the seven Christmas dates, and dogs are not permitted there. King Street is not Riverlink. Keep the lead on, bring water if you have it, and give food stalls and breakable tables a bit of space.

If you were hoping for Santa Paws photos, those are listed for the later Gasworks Plaza markets in Newstead, not for King Street.

## A November twilight in Brisbane

I cannot give you a forecast for the 27th, and I will not pretend to. Late spring in Brisbane is familiar enough: four o'clock can still be hot and sticky, the UV does not vanish when you leave the office, and storm season means an evening sky can change quickly.

Before you leave work, check the Bureau of Meteorology forecast and warnings, and glance at the radar if storms are mentioned. Then check the organiser, because weather calls and early finishes are announced there, not on recycled event pages. A compact umbrella covers sun at 4pm and rain later; water helps even after the sun drops. Know where you would step under cover if the weather turns — a stall awning is not the same as a building.

If you are sensitive to heat, the later half of the 4pm–9pm window will usually feel kinder than the first hour, at the cost of first pick.
